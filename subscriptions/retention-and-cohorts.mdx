## Subscription retention

<Frame>
  <img src="/images/subscription-retention.png" alt="Subscriptions Retention: subscription retention, MRR retention, subscription churn and MRR churn, with charts over time" />
</Frame>

**Reports → Subscriptions Retention** looks at each **complete month**: of the subscriptions active on the 1st, how many were still active at the month's end.

| Card | What it is |
|---|---|
| Subscription Retention | Share of subscriptions active on the 1st still active at the end |
| MRR Retention | The same for their MRR |
| Subscription Churn, MRR Churn | What was lost: 100% minus retention |

Cards average the months in your range. Charts show retention and churn month by month, the **Monthly Breakdown** table gives each month's figures, and **Expected Lifetime** turns your average churn into how long a subscription lasts and what it's worth over that time.

<Note>Only complete months count. Pick a range that includes at least one full month.</Note>

## Subscription cohorts

<Frame>
  <img src="/images/subscription-cohorts.png" alt="Subscription Cohort Retention: subscriptions grouped by start month and how many stayed active in each later month" />
</Frame>

**Cohorts → Subscriptions Cohorts** groups subscriptions by the month they started and follows how many stay active. Read it like a [customer cohort](/advanced/cohort-analysis/what-is-cohort-analysis): rows are starting months, columns are time since.

- **Rows**: a cohort, the subscriptions that started in that month (or week, or year).
- **Month 1**: what the cohort started with. Every later column is compared with it.
- **Month 2, 3…**: how many are still active one, two… periods after the cohort began. A subscription drops out once it has ended or been cancelled, so values only go down along a row.
- **Subscriptions / MRR**: count subscriptions, or add up the monthly recurring revenue of those still active.
- **# / %**: the numbers themselves, or the share of the cohort's starting point still active.
- **Average**: each column averaged over the cohorts old enough to reach it. In **%** mode it's the total still active divided by the total started, across those same cohorts.
- **—**: the cohort hasn't reached that period yet.

Hover any cell or column heading for an explanation. The **How to read this chart** link above the table opens the same guide in the app.

<Accordion title="Worked example">
  20 subscriptions started in March. By the start of May (two months later), 3 had been cancelled.

  | | Month 1 | Month 2 | Month 3 |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | Mar (#) | 20 | 19 | 17 |
  | Mar (%) | 100% | 95% | 85% |

  Month 3 at 85% means 17 of March's 20 subscriptions were still active two months after March began.
</Accordion>

<Tip>
  **MRR % above Subscriptions %** means the subscribers who stay are on bigger plans than those who leave. Below it, you're losing your most valuable subscribers first.
</Tip>

**What to look for**

- **The drop from Month 1 to Month 2.** A sharp one usually means trial or first-payment churn: look at onboarding and the first renewal.
- **Where a row levels off.** After that point subscribers tend to stay, so getting customers past it matters most.
- **A column read top to bottom.** If newer cohorts keep a higher share at the same age, retention is improving.

Healthy subscription businesses often keep 80% or more at month 3.
